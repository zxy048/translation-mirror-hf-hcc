## Summary of the Study

Heart failure (HF) and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) appear clinically divergent — one a chronic degenerative condition of the energy-depleted myocardium, the other an aggressive malignancy — yet both exhibit profound dysregulation of translation, the most energetically expensive step of gene expression, in opposite directions (suppression in HF, activation in HCC). Whether these opposite perturbations reflect a conserved transcriptional program or disease-specific programs converging on shared functional themes has remained unresolved.

We addressed this question using an integrative cross-disease comparative transcriptomics framework. Applying identical computational pipelines to independent HF (GSE57338, *n* = 313) and HCC (GSE141198, *n* = 148; TCGA-LIHC, *n* = 421) cohorts, we found:

1. **Disease-specific network organization.** WGCNA identified translation-related co-expression modules in both diseases — the green module in HF (227 genes, enriched for ribosome biogenesis) and the blue module in HCC (1,315 genes, enriched for cytoplasmic translation) — but with limited gene-level overlap (62/227 genes; Fisher's OR = 1.7, *P* = 0.039), indicating disease-context-dependent network configurations rather than module conservation.

2. **Mirror perturbation at the pathway level.** Cross-disease ssGSEA of 81 pathways revealed a strong negative correlation in translation-related pathway effect sizes (Spearman ρ = −0.598, *P* = 0.0003; permutation *P* = 0.0079). Twenty-four of 33 translation-related pathways were upregulated in HCC and downregulated in HF, representing a predominant mirror-direction pattern.

3. **Disease-context dependence.** The mirror perturbation was absent in same-organ disease controls (hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, ρ = −0.036; cirrhosis, ρ = +0.402), excluding a generic organ- or disease-level effect, and showed cohort-dependent variability in an independent HF cohort (GSE116250), indicating that the magnitude of remodeling depends on disease context.

4. **Regulatory correlates.** ATF4, a central transcription factor of the integrated stress response, was the strongest individual TF correlate of the translation-associated transcriptional score (TATS; ρ = +0.500, FDR < 0.0001), while MYC target pathway activity showed the highest pathway-level association (ρ = +0.753, *P* < 0.0001). De-overlap sensitivity analysis confirmed that the MYC–TATS association was not solely explained by shared gene representation.

Collectively, these findings support a **disease-context-dependent remodeling** framework: translation-related transcriptional programs are organized through distinct disease-specific networks yet converge on opposite-direction pathway perturbation — a conceptual principle distinguishing shared functional themes from conserved network architecture.

## Significance and Fit for BBA – Molecular Basis of Disease

We believe this manuscript is well-suited for BBA – Molecular Basis of Disease for the following reasons:

- **Molecular mechanism focus.** The study identifies specific molecular programs (ATF4/ISR, MYC) that converge on the translational machinery across two major diseases, providing a mechanistic framework consistent with the journal's emphasis on the molecular basis of human disease processes.

- **Cross-disease perspective.** By comparing two diseases with opposing physiological demands within a single analytical framework, we distinguish conserved organizational principles from disease-specific transcriptional responses — an approach that reveals regulatory logic invisible to single-disease analyses.

- **Translational implications.** The identification of ATF4/ISR as a stress-responsive regulatory axis and MYC as a proliferative correlate converging on translation points to candidate targetable vulnerabilities with potential therapeutic relevance.

- **Methodological rigor.** All analyses use publicly available clinical transcriptomic data with identical computational pipelines applied across diseases, ensuring full reproducibility. Analysis code is deposited in a public GitHub repository (https://github.com/zxy048/translation-mirror-hf-hcc).

Dear Editor,

We submit for consideration at *Journal of Translational Medicine* our manuscript entitled **"Distinct networks drive mirror perturbation of translation-related transcription in heart failure and hepatocellular carcinoma."**

**Why *Journal of Translational Medicine*.** Our study exemplifies the translational multi-omics framework that *JTM* has championed: we integrate transcriptomic co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), pathway activity scoring (ssGSEA), and transcription factor association analysis across two major diseases — heart failure and hepatocellular carcinoma — using identical computational pipelines across multiple independent cohorts. The central finding — that translation-related transcriptional programs exhibit disease-specific network organization yet converge on opposite-direction pathway perturbation — is not merely a cross-disease observation but a framework with translational implications: it identifies ATF4/ISR (stress-responsive) and MYC (proliferative) as complementary regulatory dimensions that may inform future therapeutic strategies targeting translational dysregulation. The multi-resolution analytical approach (network → pathway → clinical association → regulatory correlate) aligns with *JTM*'s emphasis on computationally rigorous translational research that bridges bench and bedside. No new experimental data were generated; our contribution lies in the systematic re-analysis and integration of publicly available clinical transcriptomic datasets to derive insights not apparent from single-disease studies.

**Importance and novelty.** Heart failure and cancer are conventionally studied in separate disciplinary silos, yet both exhibit profound dysregulation of translation — the most energetically expensive step of gene expression — in opposite directions (suppression in the failing heart, activation in the proliferating tumor). Whether these opposite perturbations reflect a conserved transcriptional program or disease-specific programs converging on shared functional themes has remained unknown. Using identical computational pipelines across multiple independent cohorts, we report: (1) translation-related modules exhibit disease-specific co-expression network organization with limited gene-level overlap (62/227 genes; Fisher's OR = 1.7, *P* = 0.039); (2) despite this network divergence, 24 of 33 translation-related pathways show convergent mirror perturbation at the pathway level (Spearman ρ = −0.598, *P* = 0.0003); and (3) this mirror pattern is absent in same-organ disease controls (hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, ρ = −0.036; cirrhosis, ρ = +0.402), supporting disease-context dependence rather than organ-specific confounding. We introduce the framework of **disease-context-dependent remodeling** — shared functional themes emerging through distinct network configurations with opposite perturbation directions — and identify ATF4/ISR and MYC as complementary regulatory dimensions associated with translation-related remodeling.

## Summary of the Study

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) requires sustained protein synthesis to support malignant proliferation, and translation — the most energetically expensive step of gene expression — is centrally dysregulated in this disease. Whether this activation reflects a transcriptional program shared across disease states or a context-dependent remodeling specific to HCC has remained unresolved. To answer this, we compared HCC against heart failure (HF), a disease of fundamentally opposing translational demand, using an integrative cross-disease transcriptomic framework applied to independent cohorts (HCC: GSE141198, *n* = 148 and TCGA-LIHC, *n* = 421; HF: GSE57338, *n* = 313).

We found three principal results:

1. **Disease-specific network organization.** WGCNA identified translation-related co-expression modules in both diseases — the blue module in HCC (1,315 genes, enriched for cytoplasmic translation) and the green module in HF (227 genes, enriched for ribosome biogenesis) — but with limited gene-level overlap (62/227 genes; Fisher's OR = 1.7, *P* = 0.039), indicating disease-context-dependent network configurations rather than module conservation.

2. **Mirror perturbation at the pathway level.** Cross-disease ssGSEA of 81 pathways revealed a strong negative correlation in translation-related pathway effect sizes (Spearman ρ = −0.598, *P* = 0.0003; permutation *P* = 0.0079). Twenty-four of 33 translation-related pathways were upregulated in HCC and downregulated in HF — a predominant mirror-direction pattern.

3. **Disease-context dependence with candidate regulatory correlates.** The mirror perturbation was absent in same-organ disease controls (hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, ρ = −0.036; cirrhosis, ρ = +0.402), indicating disease-context rather than tissue-level baseline differences. Among regulatory correlates, MYC target pathway activity showed the strongest association with the translation-associated transcriptional score (TATS; ρ = +0.753, *P* < 0.0001), while ATF4, a central transcription factor of the integrated stress response (ISR), was the strongest individual transcription-factor correlate (ρ = +0.500, FDR < 0.0001).

Collectively, these findings support a **disease-context-dependent remodeling** framework in HCC: translation-related transcriptional programs are organized through HCC-specific network architecture, converge on shared functional themes whose perturbation direction is shaped by disease context, and are associated with proliferative (MYC) and stress-responsive (ATF4/ISR) regulatory dimensions.

## Fit for Hepatology Communications

We believe this manuscript is well suited to Hepatology Communications for the following reasons:

- **Hepatology focus.** The study provides a systematic, quantitative characterization of translation-related transcriptional programs in HCC — a core topic in hepatocyte biology and hepatocarcinogenesis — using clinically relevant transcriptomic cohorts.

- **Integrative computational framework.** The study applies a rigorous, fully reproducible pipeline (WGCNA, ssGSEA, transcription-factor correlation analysis, permutation and de-overlap sensitivity testing) consistent with Hepatology Communications' history of publishing genomics and bioinformatics studies in liver disease.

- **Conceptual advance.** By comparing HCC with a disease of opposing translational demand within a single analytical framework, we distinguish disease-specific network organization from shared functional themes — an approach that identifies regulatory logic invisible to single-disease analyses.

- **Translational relevance.** The identification of MYC and ATF4/ISR as candidate regulatory dimensions converging on the translational apparatus nominates translation-related programs as potential targetable vulnerabilities in HCC, complementing existing molecular-targeted and immunotherapeutic frameworks.
